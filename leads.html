Your answers

It sounds like you, because it learned from you

On setup day we read through the emails you've already sent and build a list of approved answers: what you charge, when you're open, where you work, how you handle common requests. You check it, change anything you like, and that list is all it answers from.

Replies wait for your OK by default. If you approve the same kind of reply five times without changing it, you can choose to let that one kind send on its own, and switch it back off any time.

When you edit a draft, it learns. When an answer starts to look out of date, like an old price, it flags it instead of guessing. And it tells you about questions it couldn't answer, so the list keeps getting better.

Questions

About leads and replies

Will it reply to customers without asking me?

No. Every reply waits for your OK. If you approve the same kind of reply five times without changing it, you can choose to let that one kind go out on its own, and switch it back off any time.

How does it know what to say?

On setup day we build a list of approved answers from emails you've already sent: your hours, prices, service area, how you handle common requests. It only answers from that list, and flags anything that looks out of date.

What about angry customers or refunds?

Those never get an automatic answer. Complaints, refunds, billing disputes and anything that looks suspicious go straight to you.
